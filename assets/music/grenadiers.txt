================================================================================
 The British Grenadiers — Tritone arrangement (Title screen)
================================================================================
Traditional English march, 17th/18th century, PUBLIC DOMAIN.  No known author;
the setting below is transcribed from the Bacon (CJS MSS) collection,
Longborough, via abcnotation.com:

  X:391  T:39. MORRIS SET#3 (32 bar marches) / The British Grenadiers
  M:4/4  K:G  S:Bacon (CJS MSS)  A:Longborough  O:English
  P:A  D2 | G2 D2 G2 A2 | B4 A2 Bc | d2 G2 BAGF | (G4 G2) D2 | ... ::
  P:B  Bc | d3 e d2 c2 | B2 c2 d2 d2 | e2 e2 dcBA | G4 F2 D2 |
       G2FG A2GA | B2AB c2Bc | d2 G2 BAGF | (G4 G2) :|

Chosen because it is unambiguously out of copyright -- a traditional tune with
no author to have died recently -- and because a strategy game wants a march
that sounds like an army rather than a fanfare.

ARRANGEMENT
  * The A strain is written ONCE, not twice.  The ABC repeats it (::) and
    the repeat cost 279 bytes we did not have -- checkmem put _anim_beat at
    0xC117, over the ceiling.  A tune that loops forever on a title screen
    does not need its own internal repeat; the loop supplies it.
  * Key: G major => every F is F#.  Watch bars 4, 8, 13, 14, 16.
  * Meter: 4/4 with L=1/8, so 8 eighths per bar -> 8 rows, one row per eighth.
  * TWO parts, as asked: CH1 carries the tune, CH2 harmonises a third below
    and drops to sixths where a third would cross under the bass line's
    register.  CH3 IS DELIBERATELY SILENT -- a two-part melody plus drums is
    the brief, and a bass would make it three.
  * CH4 is the drum tattoo (Tritone's percussion channel; "third channel" in
    the brief means the third VOICE, since CH1-3 are all tone channels and a
    drum cannot be placed on one).

THE TATTOO, AND ITS DELAY
  A military tattoo is a rhythm played on the side drum.  Tritone gives one
  hit per row and NO volume control, so a delay cannot be done by echoing the
  same drum quieter -- it has to be echoed on a LIGHTER INSTRUMENT.  The
  pattern is therefore:

      row 0  snare   the beat
      row 1  snare   the double-tap that makes it a tattoo
      row 3  tick    <- delay, two rows after the double-tap
      row 4  kick    the half-bar
      row 6  tick    <- delay, two rows after the kick

  Two rows at speed 13 is about 0.26 s, which reads as an echo across a
  parade ground rather than as a separate note.

  Bar 1 is drums ALONE: the tune's own pickup does not arrive until row 6, and
  opening on the tattoo says "march" before a single pitch is heard.

TEMPO
  A brisk quick march.  Quarter = 115 BPM, so one eighth = 0.26 s = 13 frames.
rows  = 8
speed = 13
